# Evidence Assessment Method

## 1. Purpose

This method defines how evidence is collected, classified, traced and used throughout the independent desk-based assessment of the Málaga PIDM 2024 Public Safety module.

The purpose is to make the analysis transparent, reproducible and proportionate to the available documentation.

## 2. Evidence sources

The primary source is the technical procurement specification for the development, implementation and commissioning of the Málaga Intelligent Destination Platform (PIDM 2024), case reference EXPTE. SERV. – 125/25.

Additional sources may be considered where relevant and available. Each source must be recorded in the source register, including its origin, date, role in the assessment and limitations.

## 3. Evidence classification

Each material statement should be classified using the following categories:

- **Documented fact:** A statement explicitly supported by an identified source passage.
- **Analytical inference:** A reasoned interpretation derived from documented facts. The reasoning and supporting references must be stated.
- **Unknown:** A matter that cannot be established from the evidence reviewed.
- **Verification required:** A claim or condition that requires additional documentation, stakeholder confirmation, technical inspection or testing.

These categories must not be treated as interchangeable. In particular, a missing requirement in the reviewed documentation does not, by itself, prove that a capability or control is absent in practice.

## 4. Traceability

Material requirements, observations, risks and assurance tests should reference their supporting source IDs and, where applicable, the relevant section or page.

The analysis should preserve the distinction between what the source states, what the assessor infers and what remains unverified.

Where evidence is incomplete, ambiguous or contradictory, the limitation should be recorded rather than silently resolved.

## 5. Assessment boundaries

This portfolio is an independent, desk-based assessment of publicly available or otherwise legitimately obtained documentation.

It is not commissioned by, delivered to or endorsed by the Ayuntamiento de Málaga, the platform's suppliers or other procurement participants.

Unless additional evidence becomes available, the assessment cannot establish:

- Whether specified capabilities have been implemented or are operational.
- Whether integrations work as intended.
- Actual model accuracy, performance or error rates.
- Whether organisational and technical controls operate effectively.
- Whether the deployed system complies with every applicable legal or regulatory obligation.

No live-system access, production testing or stakeholder validation should be implied unless it has actually taken place.

## 6. Use of findings

Potential risks and control gaps should be presented as assessment observations or hypotheses proportionate to the evidence available.

Recommendations should identify the issue addressed, the rationale, the evidence supporting it and any assumptions or dependencies.

The assessment should distinguish documented non-conformity from a potential risk, an evidence gap or a recommendation for further verification.

Risk assessments must distinguish inherent risk from residual risk. Documented controls may be considered in the assessment only to the extent supported by available evidence. A requirement specified in procurement documentation does not establish that the corresponding control has been implemented or is effective. Where likelihood, control effectiveness or residual risk cannot be assessed reliably, the uncertainty and its rationale must be recorded.

## 7. Review and quality assurance

Before publication, each material finding should be checked for:

1. Source traceability.
2. Accurate representation of the source.
3. Clear separation of fact and inference.
4. Explicit limitations and unresolved questions.
5. Proportionate conclusions and actionable recommendations.
6. Consistent risk ratings, with separate rationales for impact, likelihood, control status and residual risk where applicable.
7. Consistent identifiers and valid traceability across the source, requirements, evidence-gap, risk and assurance-test registers.

Any material correction should be reflected in the relevant register and version history.
