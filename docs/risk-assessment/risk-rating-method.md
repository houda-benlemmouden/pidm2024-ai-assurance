# Risk Rating Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This methodology defines how risks identified during the independent, desk-based assessment of PIDM2024 Module #32 (Public Safety) are described, rated, prioritised and reviewed.

The method is designed to support consistent, evidence-based judgements. Ratings are analytical assessments based on the documentation available, not measurements of the actual system's operational risk.

## 2. Risk Statement

Each risk should describe:

- **Cause or condition:** The relevant system characteristic, dependency, uncertainty or potential control weakness.
- **Risk event:** What could happen.
- **Potential consequence:** The resulting impact on people, public safety, service delivery, privacy, security, accountability or other relevant interests.
- **Affected stakeholders:** The people, organisations or services that could be affected.

Risk statements must distinguish documented facts from inferences and unverified conditions. An evidence gap alone is not proof that a control is absent or that an adverse event has occurred.

## 3. Inherent Risk

Inherent risk represents the potential risk before giving credit to controls.

Each inherent risk should be assessed using two dimensions:

- **Impact:** The severity of the plausible adverse consequence.
- **Likelihood:** The estimated possibility of the risk event occurring within the relevant operational context and timeframe.

Both dimensions use a five-point scale, subject to the evidence limitations described in this methodology.

### 3.1 Impact scale

| Score | Rating | General interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Negligible | Minimal, readily reversible effect with no material disruption or harm. |
| 2 | Minor | Limited and localised effect, manageable through routine corrective action. |
| 3 | Moderate | Meaningful harm, disruption, rights impact or service degradation requiring specific intervention. |
| 4 | Major | Serious harm, substantial service disruption, significant rights impact or major accountability failure. |
| 5 | Severe | Potential for life-threatening or catastrophic harm, widespread serious consequences, or exceptionally severe and difficult-to-reverse impacts. |

Impact should reflect the most serious reasonably foreseeable consequence supported by the scenario, not the worst imaginable outcome.

Where different dimensions of impact are relevant, the rationale should identify them explicitly. A low score in one dimension must not obscure a serious potential consequence in another.

### 3.2 Likelihood scale

| Score | Rating | General interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | Exceptional circumstances would be needed; supporting evidence indicates the event is highly unlikely. |
| 2 | Unlikely | The event is possible but not expected under the assessed conditions. |
| 3 | Possible | The event is plausible under the assessed conditions. |
| 4 | Likely | The event is expected to occur in a meaningful proportion of relevant circumstances. |
| 5 | Almost certain | The event is expected to occur frequently or repeatedly under the assessed conditions. |

Likelihood ratings require a stated rationale and appropriate supporting evidence. They must not be assigned solely because a system uses AI, because a control is undocumented, or because a risk scenario is conceivable.

Where the available evidence does not support a defensible likelihood estimate, record **Not assessable – insufficient evidence**. Do not substitute a midpoint score merely to complete the calculation.

Each likelihood assessment should identify the relevant operating conditions and timeframe where these can reasonably be established. If the timeframe or operating context is unknown and materially affects the judgement, record the limitation and do not assign a numeric likelihood score unless a defensible basis remains.

## 4. Inherent Risk Score and Priority

Where both impact and likelihood can be defensibly rated:

**Inherent risk score = Impact × Likelihood**

| Score | Rating |
|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low |
| 5–9 | Moderate |
| 10–16 | High |
| 17–25 | Critical |

These bands are the project's analytical prioritisation rules. They are not presented as a universal or externally mandated risk standard.

The numeric score must be accompanied by the impact and likelihood rationales. The score alone is not an adequate explanation of the risk.

Where likelihood cannot be assessed, do not calculate a numeric score. Record the potential impact, explain the uncertainty and flag the risk for further evidence collection and prioritisation.

High potential impact combined with substantial uncertainty may justify early investigation even when a numeric score cannot be established.

## 5. Control Status and Evidence

Controls must be classified according to the evidence available. At minimum, distinguish:

- **Specified:** Required or described in the reviewed documentation.
- **Proposed:** Recommended as a possible safeguard, but not established as an existing control.
- **Implementation evidenced:** Evidence indicates that the control has been implemented.
- **Effectiveness evidenced:** Evidence indicates that the implemented control operates effectively within the scope and limitations of the evidence reviewed.
- **Unknown or unverified:** Available evidence does not establish the control's implementation or effectiveness.

These statuses are not interchangeable. A procurement requirement does not prove implementation, and implementation alone does not prove effectiveness.

For each material control, record the supporting evidence, its limitations and any further verification needed. Where a control has not been assessed, do not describe it as ineffective; its status is unverified.

## 6. Residual Risk

Residual risk is the risk remaining after relevant controls have been considered.

A residual risk rating may be assigned only when the available evidence supports a defensible assessment of the relevant controls and their effect on the risk.

Where control implementation or effectiveness is unverified, record residual risk as **Not assessable from current evidence**, as appropriate. Do not reduce an inherent risk score merely because a safeguard is specified in the procurement documentation.

Where a residual rating is possible, retain the rationale and evidence explaining how the controls affect the likelihood or impact of the risk. Any assumptions must be explicit.

## 7. Uncertainty and Confidence

Uncertainty must be recorded separately from the numeric risk score.

For each material risk, document the important limitations affecting the assessment, including missing implementation evidence, unknown operating conditions, unresolved dependencies or unavailable performance data.

Use the following evidence-confidence labels:

- **High:** Relevant, direct evidence supports the assessment, with no material unresolved limitation affecting the judgement.
- **Moderate:** Evidence supports the assessment, but some material assumptions or limitations remain.
- **Low:** The assessment relies substantially on inference, incomplete evidence or unresolved assumptions.

Confidence describes the strength of the evidence supporting the assessment; it is not a substitute for impact, likelihood or control effectiveness.

Low confidence should prompt transparent qualification and, where material, further evidence collection. It must not automatically increase or decrease the numeric score.

## 8. Risk Prioritisation and Review

Prioritisation should consider:

- The inherent risk rating where it can be calculated.
- The severity and reversibility of potential consequences.
- The stakeholders who may be affected.
- Evidence confidence and material uncertainty.
- Dependencies on other systems, organisations or controls.
- The urgency and feasibility of obtaining evidence or reducing the risk.

The numeric bands support consistency but do not replace reasoned judgement. Any decision to prioritise a risk differently from its numeric band must be explained.

Ratings should be revisited if new evidence changes the risk scenario, impact assessment, likelihood rationale or control status.

Assessment priority is separate from the numeric risk rating and describes the urgency of further assessment or evidence collection. Use **High** when potentially serious consequences or material uncertainty justify prompt investigation; **Moderate** when further assessment is needed but the available evidence does not indicate the same urgency; and **Low** when follow-up can reasonably be scheduled for routine review. Record the rationale for each priority. These labels do not represent verified operational risk or prove that harm is likely.

## 9. Limitations

This methodology supports a documentary assessment. It does not establish the actual likelihood of events, the operational effectiveness of controls, the performance of AI models, or the compliance status of a deployed system without supporting evidence.

All ratings and priorities must remain proportionate to the evidence available. Material unknowns must remain visible in the risk register and final report.
