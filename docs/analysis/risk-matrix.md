# Likelihood × Impact Risk Matrix

## Current assessment status

A conventional risk matrix plots each risk according to its assessed likelihood and impact. The current risk register records inherent impact scores, but likelihood scores have not been assessed.

For this reason, risks cannot yet be placed reliably into likelihood × impact cells. No likelihood values or overall risk scores have been inferred or invented for visualisation.

The current register contains 11 risks:

| Inherent impact score | Rating | Number of risks |
|---:|---|---:|
| 3 | Moderate | 3 |
| 4 | Major | 8 |

These values represent impact only. They must not be interpreted as calculated overall risk ratings.

## Why the matrix remains incomplete

The documented method requires impact and likelihood to be considered separately and supported by the risk scenario and available evidence.

The current assessment leaves likelihood unassessed where the reviewed documentation does not establish the information needed to estimate it reliably, including relevant operating exposure, implementation evidence, data characteristics, and control effectiveness.

Assessment priority is recorded separately and is not interchangeable with a calculated likelihood × impact rating.

## Evidence needed to complete the matrix

For each risk, the assessment would need sufficient evidence to justify a likelihood estimate and its timeframe. Depending on the scenario, that may include:

- Verified information about whether and how the relevant capability is implemented and used.
- Operating conditions, exposure, frequency, and relevant event or incident data.
- System performance and failure evidence under representative conditions.
- Evidence about relevant preventive, detective, and mitigative controls.
- A documented rationale and appropriate confidence level for each likelihood assessment.

The evidence needed will vary by risk. These items are proposed evidence needs, not claims that such evidence exists or has been reviewed.

## Limitation

This page documents why a likelihood × impact matrix is not yet populated. It does not imply that the risks are absent, low, or acceptable. Risk decisions should take account of the recorded impact, assessment priority, evidence confidence, and unresolved evidence gaps.
